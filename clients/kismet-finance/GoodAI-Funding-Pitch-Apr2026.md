## 2. The Perth AI Gap

### The National Picture

Australia's AI adoption has accelerated sharply. Key data points:

- 64% of Australian SMBs now use AI regularly (daily/weekly/monthly), up from 39% in mid-2024 — a 64% increase in under two years (AI Lab Australia, Q1 2026)
- But only 5% are "AI-enabled" — meaning AI is integrated into core operations and strategy, not just experimentation (ScaleSuite, 2026)
- 95% of organisations integrating GenAI report zero measurable ROI (MIT, 2025) — the failure rate hasn't improved even as adoption has surged
- AI is adding $21B annually to Australian GDP, projected to reach $142B by 2030 (Tech Council of Australia)
- 1M+ tech workers needed by 2030; 78% of tech leaders view AI/ML as the defining 2026 trend

The adoption rate went up. The success rate didn't. This is the gap.

### Why Perth Is Worse — And Why That's the Opportunity

Perth has structural conditions that make the AI gap more acute than in Sydney or Melbourne:

**Talent scarcity is more severe.** Perth automation engineer mean salary is $134K — 12% above national average. This isn't because Perth pays better; it's because there aren't enough people. The mining sector absorbs technical talent that in other cities would flow into tech services. The "Business Analyst translator" shortage — the people who bridge technology and business strategy — is the most acute gap in the national market, and it's worse in Perth where the services sector is smaller relative to resources.

**SME density is high and growing.** Western Australia had 260,730 actively trading businesses as of June 2025 (ABS), with 97% classified as small businesses. WA posted the fastest business growth of any state at 4.3% (10,877 new businesses), driven by population migration and relative affordability. These are overwhelmingly owner-operated businesses without internal IT capability.

**Economic pressure is accelerating.** Perth SMEs face the same margin compression as the east coast, but with a thinner talent pool to respond. Sectors with highest automation demand — finance (+high), construction (+19.3%), wholesale trade (+14.2%) — are all strongly represented in the Perth economy. The pressure to automate is real; the ability to execute is constrained.

**The advisory market is underdeveloped.** Sydney and Melbourne have deep benches of digital consultancies, AI integrators, and management consulting firms with AI practices. Perth has a handful of players, most positioned for enterprise or priced above SME reach (see Section 7). The sub-$10K automation build market is effectively unserved by credible local operators.

**Training alone isn't working.** Only 18% of employees who received AI training feel independently prepared to apply it. This matches what we see on the ground: business owners attend AI workshops, install ChatGPT, and then have no framework for connecting it to their invoicing, their review management, or their lead pipeline. They need guided implementation, not more education.

### The Timing Thesis

The Perth AI gap is a timing opportunity, not a permanent condition. Eventually, the enterprise players will move downmarket, east-coast consultancies will expand west, and internal capability will build. The window for a local, productised, SME-focused AI automation firm to establish market position is approximately 18-24 months. Good AI is already in market.

## 3. Business Model

Good AI generates revenue through three streams, each with different margin profiles and strategic value.

### Stream 1: Direct SME Automation Builds (One-Time + Retainer)

This is the core business. We build automation systems for SMEs and manage them on retainer.

**How it works:** Client identifies a pain point (missed calls, slow invoicing, no review management, manual onboarding). We scope and build a connected automation using n8n orchestration, AI agents (Gemini, Claude, OpenAI), and integration with their existing stack (Xero, Google Workspace, CRM). Build takes 1-4 weeks. Client pays a one-time build fee plus monthly retainer for ongoing management, optimisation, and support.

**Pricing (from service catalogue):**

| Tier | Build Fee | Monthly Retainer | Target Client |
|------|-----------|------------------|---------------|
| Starter | $1,500 - $3,500 | $150 - $400/mo | Micro-SME, 3-15 staff, $500K-$3M revenue |
| Growth | $3,000 - $5,500 | $300 - $550/mo | Growing SME, 10-50 staff, $2M-$15M revenue |
| Enterprise | $5,500 - $8,500 | $550 - $850/mo | Established business, 50+ staff, $10M+ revenue |

**Unit economics:**
- Average build: ~$4,500 (direct SME, blended across tiers); ~$7,000 (channel-sourced, skews toward Growth/Enterprise tiers due to larger client size)
- Average retainer: ~$400/mo
- Gross margin on builds: ~75% (primary cost is Kev's time; infrastructure costs are minimal)
- Gross margin on retainers: ~85% (monitoring + occasional optimisation; infrastructure costs ~$50-80/mo per client)
- Client lifetime value (18-month average retention): $4,000 build + ($400 x 18) = $11,200
- Most clients deploy 2-3 automations over 12 months, increasing LTV to $15,000-$25,000

**ROI for clients is proven across sectors:**
- Trades (12-staff company): 508% Year 1 ROI, 2.0-month payback
- Professional services (30-staff firm): 596% Year 1 ROI, 1.7-month payback
- Retail/hospitality (8-staff business): 391% Year 1 ROI, 2.4-month payback

### Stream 2: Channel Partnerships (Kismet as Proof of Concept)

Advisory firms — accounting practices, financial planners, CFO advisory firms — have warm relationships with exactly the clients who need AI automation. They see client financials, understand operational pain points, and hold trusted-advisor status. But they lack technical capability to deliver automation.

Good AI partners with these firms as their implementation layer. They introduce, we build.

**Kismet Finance Group is the first active channel:**
- 4 BDMs (Josh, DJ, Shane, Lea) across Perth metro
- 198 tracked leads, approximately 49 warm
- Revenue split: Kismet retains 60-100% of readiness assessment fees ($2K-$10K); Good AI retains 80-85% of implementation revenue with Kismet earning 15-20% referral margin
- Retainer revenue: Good AI retains 85%, Kismet earns 15% ongoing

**Why this scales:** Every mid-tier accounting firm, financial planning practice, and business advisory firm in Perth is a potential channel partner. The model is replicable — Kismet is the proof of concept, not the ceiling. Australia has approximately 28,000 accounting practices. Even capturing 5-10 channel partners in Perth represents significant pipeline multiplication.

**Channel economics (per partner):**
- Setup cost: ~$5K (training, collateral, integration)
- Expected throughput: 6-15 qualified leads per year per active channel
- Good AI revenue per channel-sourced client: $8,000-$20,000 Year 1
- Payback on channel setup: 1-2 clients

### Stream 3: Managed AI Services (Ongoing Automation Management)

This is the long-term margin engine. Once automation systems are built, clients need ongoing management — monitoring, optimisation, troubleshooting, upgrades as AI models improve. This is recurring revenue with high gross margins and low incremental cost per client.

**Current retainer tiers:**
- Starter: $150-$400/mo — monitoring, basic support, quarterly review
- Growth: $300-$550/mo — active optimisation, monthly reporting, priority support
- Enterprise: $550-$850/mo — dedicated account management, weekly optimisation, SLA guarantees

**Strategic value:** Retainer revenue provides revenue predictability for financial modelling, creates switching costs (clients become dependent on integrated systems), and generates expansion opportunities (clients with one automation typically add 2-3 more within 12 months).

**Revenue mix target (Year 3):**
- Builds: 35% of revenue (high but declining share as retainer base grows)
- Channel referral margins: 10% of revenue
- Managed retainers: 55% of revenue (growing share, highest margin)

## 6. Social Impact Thesis: AI as a Force Multiplier for Under-Resourced Founders

This section describes a differentiated impact angle that positions Good AI for impact investment and government grant eligibility. It is not the primary business case — it is an additional value layer.

### The Problem

Australia's First Nations business sector is growing — approximately 30,000 Indigenous businesses operate nationwide, generating $16.1B in revenue and employing 116,000 people. The sector grew more than 50% in five years (IBA/ANU research). Supply Nation's verified suppliers passed $5.83B in procurement spend in FY25, up $1.23B from the prior year.

But growth is constrained by a capability gap. Indigenous businesses and corporations employ 20% of Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander workers despite representing less than 1% of total firms. Access to business support, networks, and capital — particularly for businesses needing $50K-$100M in growth funding — is a documented barrier.

A specific manifestation: founders running multiple businesses simultaneously not by strategic choice, but because the talent and support ecosystem doesn't exist to delegate operational management. When one person runs 10 companies because they can't find or develop operational managers, that's not entrepreneurial ambition — it's a capacity problem. AI automation directly addresses this by replacing operational throughput that would otherwise require hires that don't exist.

### The Thesis

AI automation is a force multiplier for under-resourced founders. A solo operator managing multiple businesses can deploy automation for review management, content creation, invoicing, client communication, and lead nurture — recovering 20-40 hours per week of operational capacity without hiring.

This is not charity. This is commercial automation services sold to businesses that happen to face structural barriers to hiring. The ROI math is the same: 400-600% Year 1 return, 2-month payback. The social impact is a byproduct of serving an underserved market profitably.

### Why This Matters for Investment

**Impact investment eligibility:** The Social Enterprise Development Initiative (SEDI) provides grants up to $120,000 for social enterprises. The Australian Government has committed $11.6M over 3 years (2023-2026) plus $3.3M specifically for First Nations social enterprises. IBA's Social Enterprise Grant provides $50,000-$200,000 for projects supporting First Nations economic empowerment. The National Reconstruction Fund lists AI and enabling technologies as priority areas when demonstrating community benefit.

**Government grant alignment:** Good AI's automation services align with multiple federal and state programs — the AI Adopt Program ($3M-$5M grants), Industry Growth Program ($50K-$5M matched grants), SMEC AI Program (SME AI adoption centres), and WA-specific programs. Serving First Nations businesses strengthens applications across all of these.

**Market differentiation:** No AI automation firm in Perth is positioning around social impact. This is an uncontested angle that opens funding channels unavailable to pure commercial operators.

**Scale potential:** There are approximately 30,000 Indigenous businesses in Australia. Even capturing 1% = 300 clients. At $12K average annual revenue = $3.6M addressable market in this segment alone, before considering the broader under-resourced founder market.

### What This Is Not

This is not a pitch to any specific First Nations organisation or individual. It is a description of a market segment and impact thesis that Good AI intends to develop as a commercial service with social impact characteristics. Specific partnerships and engagement strategies will be developed in due course, led by genuine relationships rather than opportunistic positioning.
